# Living World — Runic Tools Research

- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Branch:** `research/runic-tools`
- **Status:** research only; no milestone, implementation task, balance numbers or final naming are committed.
- **Question:** how could Living World support powerful tree-felling / vein-mining convenience without turning them into free automation, while adding a restrained fantasy/mystical identity that still feels Minecraft-like?

## Product framing

Tree felling and vein mining are not treated as ordinary QoL in this research. They materially change resource-gathering throughput and therefore belong to a gameplay/balance system.

The working fantasy image is an **inscribed tool** that gains a special capability and carries a finite magical reserve. The capability and the fuel do not need to be the same concept:

- **Rune / inscription:** grants or defines the behavior.
- **Charge / resonance / essence:** pays for repeated magical work.

This distinction lets a rune remain a durable identity on the tool while its energy can be depleted and restored.

## Systems studied

### Hypixel SkyBlock

Relevant patterns are split across multiple systems rather than one universal rule.

- Current Foraging uses **Sweep** as a bounded extra-log mechanic. Hypixel's 2025 Foraging update explicitly introduced Sweep/Toughness and moved away from the old passive Treecapitator model.
- Mining has **Mining Spread**, a stat for extra adjacent blocks.
- Drills carry their own **Fuel** meter, can expand capacity with fuel tanks, and consume fuel when mining.
- SkyBlock also has **Mana** as a player resource for item abilities and **Soulflow** as a separate, consumable/internalized resource for a narrower family of powerful abilities.
- Runecrafting historically serves cosmetic attachment rather than being SkyBlock's primary power-resource model.

**Lesson:** Hypixel does not force every magical/utility mechanic into one universal mana pool. Tool-bound fuel, player mana, specialized currencies, stats and cooldowns coexist when they solve different progression problems.

### Skyrim

Enchanted weapons have a finite internal charge. Filled Soul Gems are consumed to recharge an enchanted weapon.

**Lesson:** this is a strong fantasy precedent for **power belongs to the artifact; an external mystical material restores it**. It keeps the item's magical identity separate from the player's general stamina/magicka.

### Old School RuneScape

Magic normally consumes rune reagents. Some charged weapons, such as the Trident of the Seas, can be loaded with a recipe of runes/currency, store thousands of charges and consume one charge per built-in cast.

**Lesson:** several world materials can be converted into a compact item-bound reserve. This creates an explicit economy and avoids requiring the player to carry the full reagent recipe for every individual use.

### Diablo II: Resurrected

Runes and Runewords are primarily **permanent item-construction language**: specific rune combinations in compatible socketed items produce defined powers.

**Lesson:** "rune" works especially well as the thing that **defines what the item does**, not necessarily the fuel burned every time it acts.

### Botania

Botania treats mana as world/infrastructure energy. Mana is generated by systems, stored in Mana Pools, transported, and can also be carried in portable Mana Tablets. Tools such as the Terra Shatterer interact with mana and can store large quantities internally.

**Lesson:** world-generated magical energy can create strong fantasy identity, but it also creates infrastructure, progression and automation commitments far larger than two utility tools.

### Hex Casting

Hex Casting uses **amethyst-derived media** as magical payment. Magic items can contain an internal battery, be depleted, and be recharged with amethyst-based resources.

**Lesson:** this is particularly compatible with Minecraft's existing material language. A mundane vanilla resource becomes a magical conductor/fuel without adding an unrelated RPG bar to the player.

### FTB Ultimine / modern vein-miner implementations

FTB Ultimine exposes independent limits/costs such as maximum blocks, exhaustion per extra block, optional XP cost, cooldown and valid-tool requirements. Other server-side vein-miner implementations commonly require an explicit activation and charge tool durability for additional blocks.

**Lesson:** bulk breaking needs multiple independent safety dimensions. A magical fuel alone should not replace hard maximum-size, tool-validity, protection and durability rules.

### Terraria / Elden Ring / generic player-mana systems

Terraria magic weapons consume a regenerating character mana pool. Elden Ring weapon skills are attached to armaments but generally spend player FP.

**Lesson:** player mana works well when magic is already a broad character-build system with many unrelated abilities. Introducing a player-wide mana pool only for Vein Mining and Tree Felling would force regeneration, maximum-mana progression, HUD and cross-feature ownership decisions before Living World has enough magical content to justify them.

### Path of Exile charges/flasks

PoE uses both temporary character charges and item flask charges. Flask charges are spent on use and regained through gameplay.

**Lesson:** action-generated recharge can create satisfying loops, but it can also turn the recharge rule into the activity players optimize/grind. That is useful inspiration for later progression, not an obvious first default for Living World.

## Minecraft-native material language

Several vanilla materials already carry useful thematic associations:

- **Amethyst:** crystals, chimes and literal vibration resonance with sculk sensors. It is renewable after finding a geode and already reads as mystical without new worldgen.
- **Lapis lazuli:** the established enchanting-table reagent.
- **Experience:** the established player payment for enchanting/anvil work.
- **Echo Shards / sculk:** strong memory/death/ancient-city identity, but too scarce or progression-heavy to make good routine fuel.
- **Copper:** visually useful as a conductor/binding material, but its magical meaning is weaker than amethyst/lapis and should not be invented without a clear gameplay need.

A promising hypothesis is to use amethyst as the **resonant conductor/battery material**, lapis/XP as part of inscription or attunement, and reserve echo/sculk materials for rarer future rune families rather than routine recharge.

## Resource-model comparison

### A. Player mana

**Model:** player has one regenerating magical bar; tools spend it.

**Strengths**
- familiar RPG language;
- easily shared by many future spells;
- supports character-build progression.

**Costs**
- introduces global player state, regeneration policy, HUD/readability and balance ownership;
- tool swapping bypasses item-specific resource identity;
- premature if Living World has only two magical utility abilities.

**Research stance:** defer unless Living World later proves it has a broad enough magic ecosystem to deserve a character resource.

### B. Tool-bound runic charge / resonance

**Model:** rune grants capability; the individual tool stores finite energy; extra work consumes that energy.

**Strengths**
- strongest fit with Skyrim, Hypixel Drill Fuel, charged OSRS weapons and Hex Casting;
- easy to inspect on the item;
- no global HUD required;
- naturally makes enchanted tools feel like artifacts with history/value;
- trade/drop/storage preserve the exact charged item;
- cost scales directly with the convenience used.

**Costs**
- requires an explicit recharge interaction/economy;
- item metadata must be durable and duplication-safe;
- balance depends strongly on recharge material availability.

**Research stance:** strongest first-system candidate.

### C. World/network mana

**Model:** world structures generate/store magical energy; tools draw or are recharged from them.

**Strengths**
- strongest long-term fantasy/world identity;
- can compose with environment, seasons and places;
- encourages meaningful magical infrastructure.

**Costs**
- becomes a major progression/automation system;
- risks coupling ecology/environment into a utility feature;
- invites chunk/state/persistence and network-design work.

**Research stance:** potentially excellent future Living World magic, but too large as the prerequisite for first runic tools.

### D. Consumable reagent on every activation

**Model:** each use directly consumes amethyst/lapis/rune item from inventory.

**Strengths**
- simple accounting;
- visible cost;
- no stored charge metadata.

**Costs**
- inventory friction;
- feels like ammunition rather than an enchanted artifact;
- undermines the convenience the feature is meant to provide.

**Research stance:** better as recharge input than per-use payment.

## Strongest working hypothesis

Do **not** make "rune charge" synonymous with the rune itself.

A cleaner vocabulary/model is:

- **Rune:** a durable inscription installed on a compatible tool.
- **Resonance** (working name): a finite energy reserve stored in that exact tool.
- **Recharge material/ritual:** restores resonance.
- **Activation:** explicit player intent, probably sneak + normal block break for a server-side-first implementation.

Example conceptual item:

```
Netherite Pickaxe
Efficiency V
Fortune III
Rune of Veins
Resonance: 41 / 64
```

When resonance reaches zero, the pickaxe remains a normal fully functional vanilla pickaxe; only the runic propagation becomes unavailable.

This is a hypothesis, not committed terminology or balance.

## Balance invariants worth preserving

Regardless of the final fantasy economy:

1. The normally broken first block remains vanilla.
2. Every **additional successful block** is the unit that should normally consume magical charge.
3. Additional blocks should also consume ordinary tool durability through the same semantics as a real player break.
4. Fortune/Silk Touch/drop/XP behavior should stay vanilla through Paper's player-break API rather than being recreated manually.
5. A hard maximum block count/radius exists independently from available charge.
6. Propagation is bounded and preferably presented as a short wave rather than one unbounded synchronous burst.
7. If a protection plugin cancels an attempted block break, Living World does not bypass it; failed blocks should not become bridges through protected space.
8. The system never guarantees bonus yield per block. Its reward is saved interaction/time, not free resources.
9. Empty charge fails gracefully back to vanilla behavior.
10. No automatic background recharge until a future design explicitly proves it is desirable.

Paper 26.3's `Player.breakBlock(Block)` is a promising adapter boundary because Paper documents that it uses the main-hand item, respects enchantments, handles durability/drops/XP, fires `BlockBreakEvent`, and can fail when a third-party plugin cancels the break.

## Vein rune — lower-risk first proof

Vein Mining is the safer first behavior because eligible targets can be tightly constrained to ore families.

Research direction:

- rune only on pickaxes;
- explicit activation;
- connected ore family only;
- start with six-face adjacency rather than diagonals;
- bounded maximum candidates;
- ore/deepslate variants may belong to the same explicitly defined family;
- each extra successfully broken ore costs resonance and durability;
- stop/avoid crossing a failed protected block;
- process in bounded pulses for presentation/performance.

The precise ore-family list, maximum size, charge cost and level progression are intentionally undecided.

## Tree rune — higher-risk second proof

Tree Felling is harder because player buildings use logs.

A safe Living World implementation should recognize a plausible **natural tree**, not merely connected logs. Candidate evidence includes:

- supported trunk/log material;
- bounded trunk/canopy geometry;
- nearby leaves with natural/non-persistent leaf state where applicable;
- enough canopy evidence before activation;
- hard spatial/block limits;
- fail closed when structure classification is ambiguous.

Initial research leans toward breaking **logs only** and allowing vanilla leaf decay, instead of directly harvesting the whole canopy. That avoids inflating sapling/apple/stick output and preserves a more natural visual aftermath.

Tracking every player-placed log persistently is not currently justified; it would create storage/migration/history responsibilities just to support one feature. Prefer a bounded natural-tree classifier first and accept false negatives over destructive false positives.

## Progression ideas to investigate later

These are options, not commitments:

- rune tiers increase maximum propagation, charge capacity, or efficiency;
- tool material may or may not influence capacity;
- one "major utility rune" per tool could preserve tool identity and avoid an early socket framework;
- rune application might use a custom data-driven enchantment, a rune item + anvil/smithing interaction, or a bespoke enchanting-table interaction;
- recharge could consume amethyst directly or a crafted resonant reagent;
- XP/lapis may fit better as **attunement/application cost** than as routine per-block fuel;
- rare sculk/echo materials could unlock advanced runes rather than power ordinary daily usage;
- environmental/world resonance could become a later recharge bonus only if a broader Living World magic system emerges.

## Questions that must be answered before implementation

1. Is **Resonance** the desired fantasy language, or should the stored resource use another name?
2. Is amethyst the routine recharge material, only a conductor/core, or unrelated?
3. How is a rune obtained: enchanting table, loot/exploration, crafting, smithing/anvil, ritual, or a combination?
4. Can a tool hold exactly one Living World rune, or can multiple runes coexist?
5. Does a rune have levels? If so, what scales: max chain, charge capacity, per-block cost, or more than one?
6. Is charge spent only on additional successfully broken blocks? Current research strongly favors yes.
7. Does recharge happen anywhere from inventory materials, or only through a physical world interaction/station?
8. Should runic tools be tradeable with their stored charge intact?
9. Which trees count as safely natural, especially jungle, dark oak, acacia, mangrove and giant fungi?
10. What should happen when an operation meets a protected/unbreakable block: stop the whole wave or only stop that branch?
11. Is a short cooldown still useful for feedback/anti-spam once charge and hard limits already exist?
12. At what point would enough future magic justify adding player mana, and how would existing resonance interact with it rather than being replaced?

## Research conclusion

The strongest evidence does **not** point toward a player-wide mana bar as the first implementation.

The most coherent first architecture is:

**durable rune = capability**
+
**tool-bound finite resonance = fuel**
+
**explicit world/material recharge**
+
**normal durability + hard propagation limits**
+
**Paper/vanilla break semantics**

This preserves Minecraft's item-centric feel, gives Living World room for fantasy, creates a real balancing cost, and leaves the door open for a broader magic system later without requiring one prematurely.

No final system name, recipes, numbers, rune acquisition, recharge rule, M22 milestone or implementation task is approved by this research document.
